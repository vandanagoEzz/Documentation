# Operations Manager: Responsibilities and Accountability

Last updated: 2026-10-19. Owner: Vandana Mishra (founder).

## Role summary

The Operations Manager makes sure goEasy always has enough vetted drivers to cover the rides parents book, and that every booked ride is fulfilled on time with a satisfied parent.

**Primary outcome:** a sufficient supply of vetted drivers to meet demand.

The role owns four things: checking driver applicants and moving them through the pipeline, estimating how many drivers goEasy needs and in which areas, onboarding drivers through goEasy's multipoint vetting, and fulfilling bookings.

## What this role is accountable for

The role is judged on whether driver supply keeps up with demand. Lead measures are what the Operations Manager does each day and week. Lag measures show whether it worked.

**Lead measures (the actions the role controls)**

| Measure | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Unblock drivers | Every day, resolve any document upload problem |
| Driver interviews | Interview 5 drivers a day from areas goEasy serves or wants to expand into |
| Pipeline calls | Call 10 drivers a day to move them through the pipeline |
| Onboarding time | Reduce to 2 weeks |
| Process and technology | Document the process and make sure the technology supports it |

**Lag measures (the results)**

| Measure | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Serviced rides per week | Increase |
| Driver satisfaction | Improve |
| Driver retention | Improve |

**Success in 90 days**

1. More than 10 driver applicants a week from areas goEasy serves.
2. Fewer calls from drivers asking how goEasy works.
3. Fewer calls from drivers stuck in onboarding.
4. Three drivers onboarded per week.
5. goEasy can service 100 rides a day.

## Responsibilities by cadence

The daily items are non-negotiable. Each row shows what the role does and what it produces.

| Cadence | Responsibility | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily | Process driver applicants: review eligibility, interview with 3 to 5 questions, get every document completed, and start the background check | Applicants moved forward, with the tracker updated |
| Daily | Check how many rides today, this week and next week have no driver. Confirm the right drivers are matched, match more if needed, and text or call drivers when required | Coverage gaps closed, or listed |
| Daily | Report the rides that can't be fulfilled and give a plan for avoiding it next time | Daily report of unfulfilled rides and the strategy |
| Daily | Unblock drivers with document upload problems, interview 5 drivers, and call 10 drivers | Lead measures met (see above) |
| Weekly | Every Tuesday, report how many drivers moved through the pipeline in the past week | Tuesday pipeline report |
| Weekly | Document the process as it is used | Updated process notes |
| Monthly | Monthly report on driver supply and ride fulfillment | Monthly report |
| Monthly | Keep the standard operating procedure (SOP) documented and current | SOP |
| Monthly | Talk with customers (parents) as an additional responsibility | Customer conversations and notes |

## Decision rights

The Operations Manager decides driver payout and schedules meetings without asking. Two situations go to the founder.

| Decides alone | Escalate to the founder |
| --- | --- |
| Driver payout for covering a ride | A driver should move through the pipeline, but the system rejects them and it is unclear what to do |
| Scheduling meetings with drivers and the team | A ride is not covered and the payout needed is more than the cost of the ride |

## Standards and behaviors

- Be polite when speaking with drivers and parents.
- Never skip a vetting checkpoint when onboarding a driver.
- Do not repeat the same mistake twice.
